**Source:** @tcpwithjosh (TheConversationPitwithJosh), TikTok, 2026-07-17 · 552K views, 36.1K likes, 2.2K comments, 6K reposts · fetched 2026-07-28
**BS score: 9/10 — Real astronomy vocabulary stitched onto a fabricated cosmology; the core claims are contradicted by all-sky survey data and basic orbital mechanics.**

## What it says (neutral summary)

Using fruit as props, the speaker explains that computer models of the solar system "don't add up," which he attributes to a second, undiscovered star that forms a binary pair with our Sun. He claims the two stars cross paths roughly every 33,000 years (last time ~13,000 years ago), that these crossings smash planets — creating the asteroid belt from a destroyed planet — and that the "black ash layer" and Younger Dryas impact theory record the last crossing. He adds that a planet beyond the gas giants and Mars both once hosted life, Mars ending via "internal destruction with the people that resided on that." The video description ties it to Nibiru, the Nemesis hypothesis, and Zecharia Sitchin's Anunnaki theories.

## Hype signals observed

- **Hedge-free confidence about the unproven:** "Now, this is true. We do have a binary star system. They've just yet to find it." Zero hedging on a claim excluded by an all-sky survey.
- **Unrealistic specificity:** "roughly every 33,000 years… 13,000 years ago… another 20,000 years" — precise numbers with no source, implying rigor without providing any.
- **Built-in unfalsifiability:** history books show nothing "because there isn't anything left"; telescopes can't see it "because it's moving away." Every possible disconfirmation is pre-explained.
- **Real science as garnish:** elliptical orbits, the Sun's barycentric wobble, the Kuiper belt, and the Younger Dryas hypothesis are all real — and all stitched onto claims they don't support. This is the video's core mechanism.
- **"Go and Google it"** substituting for sources — the only citation in seven minutes.
- **Cliffhanger lore:** Mars civilization destroyed by its inhabitants is "another story for another day" — serialized mystery mechanics, not explanation.

## Bottom line

The genuinely true parts — orbits are elliptical, the Sun wobbles around the solar system's barycenter, astronomers really are searching for a ninth planet — are Wikipedia-level facts used as scaffolding for an invented story. The load-bearing claims all fail: the Planet Nine search is driven by Kuiper-belt orbit clustering, not a broken solar wobble; a companion star on the described orbit would be blazingly obvious in infrared and is ruled out by NASA's WISE survey; the asteroid belt was never a planet; and there is no evidence of any civilization on Mars. The 33,000-year catastrophe cycle appears to be an invention, resembling Sitchin's long-debunked Nibiru mythology with fresher numbers. A viewer should take away exactly one thing: when someone says "the maths doesn't add up, and I know what's missing," check whether the people who actually did the maths agree the thing is missing — here, they've looked, and it isn't there.
